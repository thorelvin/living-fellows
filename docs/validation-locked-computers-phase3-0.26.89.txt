Telephone callback
------------------
A same-building search runs only with the screen open: at most 128 squares
per update and 4,096 total, own floor before either adjacent floor. It stores
real telephone coordinates/sprite and room, never a persistent native reference.
Missing phones fall back to the original word puzzle and remain unseen in the
Director. No unbounded building survey is introduced.

Place call explicitly starts the internal line. The original ring is two
seconds on/four off, at most ten cycles; each cycle emits native world noise
at radius 18/volume 60. An active call survives closing the CRT, stops on loss
of power/removal or a changed internal line, and pauses with the game. No call
is saved or restarted on reload. A muted/no-audio client can still answer.

The context option belongs to the exact nearby reachable telephone. It uses
the existing native queued installation/action adapter for a short handset
action, revalidating actor, object, program identity, line, access and power.
A subtitle panel repeats the seeded six-digit code. One in five machines also
reads a real last-entry excerpt. Heard state persists; a wrong typed code then
exposes the repetition. The ten-game-minute redial delay is five at Electrical 3.

County Key Escrow
-----------------
Native radio channel 96.4 MHz, fixed source 12000,9000 with range 100,000.
No transmission originates at the player. One 03:00 crossing per night is
recorded; fast-forward never produces a backlog. At most three queued serials
are broadcast, with six real seconds between lines and the key repeated.
Unheard serials rotate fairly, leave the queue after fourteen unheard nights,
and register again when their computer is reopened. The pending list caps at 16.

Only a matching native reception event through a powered, audible receiver
records a heard key. Sending, unrelated events and repeated callbacks do not.
Eight-digit seeded serials distinguish queue entries; six-digit keys are typed
at the computer. Wrong input exposes a key only if it was heard. The third
heard-call history triggers the real player's forename once per world. The
original five-note UI signature plays on verified receipt. EMMA has the supplied
automated-station answer.

World hearing receipts cap at 4,096, covering every computer in the supplied
base-map census. Hearing while the matching screen is open also commits the
receipt to that computer; returning to a heard computer copies the receipt into
its own saved record. No time-based key expiry is introduced. Normalization,
queue collision checks and all lists remain bounded.

USER2
------
Eligibility uses actual undiscovered, non-destroyed recruitable Strange Folk
groups with real known coordinates. Recruited groups are excluded. The saved
group ID is resolved again, not replaced with invented people. If no one is
eligible the Director skips this lock. Destroyed/missing groups cannot leave
an existing race permanently locked.

The native-shaped word puzzle remains intact. USER2 considers the player's
and its own actual likeness results; its guesses do not consume player tries.
The first wait is fifteen real seconds, later waits fifteen to twenty-five.
It cannot choose the answer before its fifth guess. Remaining delay and up to
sixteen guesses persist, advance only on the open race page, and pause with
the game. Player lockout does not stop USER2. The latest guess/likeness stays
in the fixed feedback area and its word is underlined; full history scrolls.

Either winner opens the computer and reveals the same real group on the map.
A USER2 win pays half the tier-three XP and one lower reward tier. An arrival
bark referring to the race is optional polish and is not claimed implemented.
Each character remains owned by its existing faction/encounter system.
