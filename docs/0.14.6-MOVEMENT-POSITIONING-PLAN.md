# 0.14.6 movement and positioning implementation

This is the recovered implementation specification for the eight-step movement plan. It turns the playtest requirements—where companions stand, how they move, conversation distance, multi-companion spacing, and human animation under speech or stress—into auditable code and acceptance tests.

## 1. One owner for actor movement

`SCDecision` remains the sole high-level arbiter. Follow, tactical, danger, medical, downtime, and the new conversation state compete as candidates; emergency survival states outrank social movement. `SCPositioning` produces targets and posture, `SCNavigation` owns paths and collision decisions, and `SCNativeActions` alone executes native motion/animation. No positioning code sends movement, speech, crouch, facing, or emotes to the local player.

Acceptance: a conversation emits `conversation_pose` only on the registered companion; danger cancels it before another social intent.

## 2. Stable direction-relative follow formation

Up to eight followers receive deterministic slots by stable companion ID. Slot coordinates are local to the player's travel direction rather than fixed world axes. Actual recent motion wins over aiming direction. A stationary aim turn is ignored for 2.5 seconds, preventing the group from orbiting whenever the player looks around; a sustained new heading eventually rotates the formation.

Acceptance: two known IDs occupy opposite slots behind an east-moving player, retain them through a brief aim turn, and rotate after a sustained heading change.

## 3. Personal space and deterministic right-of-way

Desired formation/social squares and immediate navigation steps use short reservations. Companions do not enter a square occupied by a teammate or the player. Equal-priority conflicts use stable IDs, while rescue/combat/retreat can pre-empt routine follow movement. A persistent blockage produces one bounded lateral yield step and a repath instead of two actors pushing through each other forever.

Acceptance: an occupied next square produces `holding_personal_space`, then `right_of_way_yield`; two simultaneous claims produce `yielding_right_of_way` for one actor.

## 4. Arrival hysteresis and anti-orbit hold

Formation arrival is measured against the assigned slot—not against player distance. Entering the hold state uses a 0.9-tile band and leaving it uses a wider 1.55-tile band. This Schmitt-trigger behaviour removes rapid stop/repath toggling and the small-circle walk seen in playtest.

Acceptance: an actor at its assigned square holds; it does not release until it crosses the larger boundary.

## 5. Tactical corners, stairs, and choke flow

The existing bounded navigation remains authoritative: a companion observes an unseen corner or stair landing, moves slowly while facing the concealed route, reserves stair chokes, spaces the group, and retains an exterior/entry trail for retreat. General personal-space reservations now complement—rather than replace—the stricter stair rules. Urgent retreat and rescue remain allowed to override routine courtesy.

Acceptance: existing blind-corner, stair-facing, stair-spacing, breadcrumb, and exterior-egress tests remain green alongside the new reservation tests.

## 6. Conversation zone and partner facing

A conversation creates a five-second, non-persistent social engagement. The companion stays or approaches a free ring position 1.2–2.8 tiles from the player, stops there, and faces the player. It does not shuffle when already inside the zone, does not crowd another companion's social position, and immediately relinquishes the state when danger appears.

Acceptance: near conversations stop and face; far/overlapping conversations receive a ring target; danger cancels the engagement.

## 7. Human speech and stress body language

The first settled conversation pulse executes one atomic `conversation_pose`: face the partner and play exactly one allowlisted Build 42 human emote. Later pulses only maintain facing, preventing animation spam. Stress selects calm, uneasy, or shaken posture; highly stressed nearby followers use guarded sneak locomotion, while distance catch-up still overrides it. Zombie animation names remain forbidden by the static gate.

Acceptance: native execution verifies both facing and one human emote; the next pulse has no emote; high stress returns guarded sneak mode.

## 8. Bounded diagnostics, reset, and regression coverage

All new state is transient and actor-keyed. Reservations expire in 450–650 ms and sweep every three seconds; conversation expires after five seconds. Reset removes actor state and reservations. `Positioning.debug` exposes only slot, target, hold, and conversation status for playtest diagnosis. No new tick hook, global zombie query, persistence schema, or bridge protocol is introduced.

Acceptance: Kahlua compiles every Lua file; gameplay, static, core/native, UI, packaging, and installer-transaction gates pass. Live playtest still verifies visual quality and engine pathing under real crowds.

## Live acceptance checklist

1. Walk and jog with two or more followers in all eight directions; identities should stay on the same side and never circle while the player merely aims.
2. Stop, turn briefly, and resume; followers should hold, then rotate only when the new travel heading is established.
3. Pass through one-tile doors and stairs with three companions; they should queue without stacking and the trailing actor should yield laterally if a normal corridor blockage persists.
4. Start conversations while adjacent, overlapping, and several tiles away; companions should settle in the ring, face the player, gesture once, and never move/crouch the player.
5. Repeat at low, medium, and high stress; only validated human gestures/locomotion should appear.
6. Introduce a zombie during conversation; combat/retreat must immediately take ownership.
7. Watch for small circles, side swapping, repeated gestures, player shouts/crouches, recurring errors, or measurable new stutter.
