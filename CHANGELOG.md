# Changelog

## 0.22.22 - Responsive work routes

- Started fixed gathering, storage, building and needs movement through a validated straight-route fast path before allocating A*. Successful obstructed companion work trips now populate a bounded, coordinate-only session cache with nearby suffix joining, first-edge/live per-step revalidation and ordinary resumable A* fallback. Combat, survival, moving targets and stealth routing bypass this cache.
- Added navigation counters for stationary fast-path hits, work-route lookup/record/repair/invalidation, A* starts/nodes/yields/completion and request-to-first-motion latency. The movement recorder now reports route strategy, expanded nodes, planning/first-motion time and shared work-cache use.

## 0.22.21 - Maximum response and performance

- Added protocol-8 main-thread bulk reads for scalar item capture, topology facts and coherent zombie snapshots, with strict Lua fallback and no game/Kahlua objects on worker threads.
- Replaced allocation-heavy protected Java calls with 0-3 argument fast paths plus successful Java-metatable method caches, reset on runtime/bootstrap generation changes. Decision rounds now share a synchronous spatial read batch.
- Made scheduled world saves resumable and atomic: 0.75 ms background slices, before/after inventory/equipment identity verification, two churn retries, a five-second deadline and preservation of the prior complete document. `OnSave` remains fresh and synchronous.
- Made roster refresh resumable and atomic on a fixed 50 ms background pulse, preserved live Status/Orders/Loadout controls across value changes, cached render text/color work and kept Debug/Factions refresh explicit. Save/UI slices keep their bounded cadence under load while remaining behind combat/survival. Optional fine-grained native/decision/item/topology/UI profiling remains off by default.
- Made tree trunk squares non-passable and kept clearance penalties on their neighbours, so Lua routing agrees with the native player pathfinder instead of repeatedly driving companions into a tree.
- Preserved vanilla fence/window root motion for companions and accepted a verified boundary crossing even when the animation lands beyond the nominal destination square. Terminal traversal records now outlive the exit-clearance window, and climb speech follows the observed result rather than only the initial roll.
- Separated ordinary catch-up speed from survival-urgent navigation. Followers still keep running briefly after the player slows, but retain room, hazard and traffic policy; an unreachable Stealth catch-up now returns to sneaking after a bounded commitment.
- Validated continuous follow steering along the actor's actual swept line, bounded open-door fallback attempts, and preserved the final portal edge when erasing a loop from the player's breadcrumb trail.
- Allowed scavengers to use proven-open diagonal corner contact while requiring the actor to occupy an actual interaction square, preventing both skipped corner counters and through-wall looting.
- Strengthened work and trade recovery: exact container identity is independent of the 256-item routine scan, proven destination deliveries can leave quarantine, dormant restored trade owners get a fresh recovery window, and terminal trade quarantines have bounded retention.
- Kept multi-worker gathering active when one resident leaves duty, made candidate cooldown identities unique under FIFO trimming, and prevented dormant registry records from masquerading as live actors.
- Documented the bridge's synchronous wall-climb outcome context and made its guard volatile.

Successful route memory is now a cost-neutral A* tie-break instead of a negative edge discount; failure memory remains a positive penalty, and a deterministic Dijkstra comparison guards shortest-path correctness.
- Replaced the companion stomp fallback's guaranteed execution with a graded Build 42 impact model. A floor attack still waits for the native `AttackCollisionCheck`, but unverified placement is now a body hit rather than an assumed head hit, and the fallback never calls `setHealth(0)`. Weapon floor attacks rely exclusively on native collision damage, while the preflight-selected BareHands weapon supplies only the stomp fallback's hit contract. Native damage still suppresses all fallback damage, and the movement recorder reports the last stomp's source, zone, damage, critical result, footwear, and health change for playtest tuning.
- Fixed three movement/action failures found in the latest playtest log. A stalled logistics visual now has an active supervisor watchdog and rolls back instead of pinning the companion indefinitely, while newly gifted weapons such as a BreadKnife stay immediately accessible in root inventory. Open gates remain classified as doors even after their collision opens, and low or tall hoppable fences now trigger Build 42's native player fence/wall climb states from a perpendicular approach. Open-space A* now uses corner-safe eight-directional routing with an octile heuristic, removing the four-direction stair-step zig-zag without allowing diagonal corner cuts through walls, doors, fences, vehicles, or occupied geometry.
- Added an opt-in base-layout overlay. The Base view can now draw nearby zones as color-coded isometric ground boundaries and mark loaded storage containers with category-colored outlines and small labels; per-record controls emphasize one zone or container. Zone creation always shows a live two-corner preview, including red feedback when a non-area zone would extend outside camp. Rendering is restricted to the player's current floor and a 48-tile radius, uses a lightweight coordinate-only read model, caches object resolution, and clears every owned outline when disabled or leaving the game.

Stable doctrine, equipped-weapon, and personality signals choose a point fighter, assault members, protected ranged support, and a rear guard; that order now controls open formation placement and the existing one-at-a-time door/stair passage queue. The formation remains in column until the final nearby member clears the portal, pauses briefly before fanning out, and the rear guard keeps a low-frequency watch behind the squad. The Squad tab and movement recorder expose each assignment for playtest diagnosis.
- Added a deterministic multi-companion AI response harness covering ordinary, fully critical, and mixed parties at 1, 4, 8, and 16 companions. The decision dispatcher now materializes and sorts the registry only once per callback, caches critical classification across both lanes, services a newly critical actor immediately, predicts non-preemptible decision cost before starting another actor, and prevents critical actors from duplicating work through the ordinary lane. In the modeled 16-companion profiles, ordinary response is bounded to 80 ms, all-critical response to 48 ms, and ordinary response under mixed critical pressure to 80 ms, with dispatch p95 at or below 1.878 ms.
- Added persistent bandit camps as a second faction archetype. Rare camps begin appearing after day four under separate sandbox limits, scale from melee groups toward occasional armed leaders as the world ages, fight zombies through the shared combat engine, patrol without abandoning their camp, challenge visible player-group members before attacking, investigate hostile sounds, remember only the last seen square, and cannot see or track targets through walls or floors. The Factions and Debug views expose the camp type, threat tier, patrol state, manual spawn/loadout controls, and engagement controls without offering household trade, contracts, or recruitment.
- Prevented a live companion from being destroyed and reconstructed when Build 42 momentarily dropped it from its square's moving-object list. The existing integrity pulse now restores that exact actor's missing square membership before the health gate runs; the health gate also classifies both forms of world-placement loss as recoverable and will retain a recruit for another in-place retry instead of sending it through persistence respawn. This keeps the same body, clothing, inventory, and identity throughout the incident.
- Fixed removed companions leaving stationary shadows with old speech bubbles. Native teardown now clears the retained line with Build 42's valid empty-string contract (passing `null` threw inside `ChatUtility` before changing the line), clears the actor-owned speech refresh and every player chat channel, explicitly removes the actor from the cell update scheduler, and verifies all of them are gone before releasing bridge ownership. Any partial cleanup remains owned and retryable instead of reporting a false success and orphaning a render/chat shell.
- Fixed a follower continuing along its old straight native path while a changed moving goal was being recalculated. Releasing a native movement lease now first cancels `PathFindBehavior2` on goal change, timeout, or failure, and every follow-route cache uses the tighter moving-target drift threshold consistently, so the actor stops before yielding to the replacement search.
- Reorganized the companion UI around five stable primary tabs: Status, Orders, Squad, Loadout, and More. Status is now a short situation/relationship summary with essential conversation; deeper personal topics live in Journal, squad signals live in Squad, and Base plus private Debug tools live behind More with a visible return path. The world menu now uses one Living Fellows root, gives the selected companion at most two context-sensitive shortcuts, hides base construction outside camp, and asks for confirmation before dismissal. Manual NPC emote controls were removed from player-facing menus; contextual automatic emotes are unchanged.
- Made bandaging verify the wound actually changed before spending the dressing. The apply step treated a successful call to the game's bandage function as proof the wound was treated, then consumed the bandage -- so if the call returned without applying (an edge case with some item interactions), the dressing was wasted with no effect. It now reads the wound back and only spends the bandage once the part is actually bandaged, otherwise it rolls back and keeps the item. This shared check now covers both a companion treating itself and you hand-bandaging a companion.
- Fixed a companion's "last thing I did" record turning into meaningless text after a reload. The most recent downtime activity (its kind, label, and time) was saved by converting the whole record to a raw string, so after loading it showed as an internal placeholder instead of the activity. It is now saved and restored with its fields intact, and older saves that stored a plain string still work.
- Fixed non-emergency companions being starved of decisions when the whole squad is in a sustained emergency. The critical (grabbed/threatened) lane could consume the entire per-frame decision budget, leaving a calm companion with no turns at all. A small guaranteed ordinary turn is now reserved each beat -- given to a genuine non-emergency companion -- so it keeps thinking and moving even while others are fighting, without slowing the emergency responses.
- Fixed a group order that fails partway through leaving a companion in an inconsistent state. When one companion in a group order couldn't be committed, the others were rolled back -- but a base-duty companion could already have been released from its duty (order says base duty, duty roster says off), and a companion's work-job details (job kind and barricade side) weren't restored. The base-duty release now happens only after the whole group order succeeds, and the work-job details are included in the rollback, so a failed group order leaves every companion exactly as it was.
- Fixed a guarding or staying companion forgetting its assigned post after a reload. The guard/stay spot was only kept in the companion's live mod-data, which a reloaded companion doesn't carry, so after loading it treated wherever it currently stood as its post -- a guard displaced by combat when you saved would hold the wrong position. The post is now saved as part of the order and restored, so a reloaded guard returns to the spot you assigned.
- Hardened companion recovery so a companion whose native body keeps failing right after each recovery can't loop forever. The recovery introduced earlier now tracks how many times a specific companion has been retired and recovered; once that passes a ceiling it is quarantined (still saved and manually recoverable) instead of endlessly re-spawning, and a companion that stays healthy for a while has that history cleared. Recovery also no longer mistakes a companion whose cleanup is still finishing for a fully recovered one, so it doesn't drop the recovery early or risk a duplicate.
- Fixed a companion's real order and its "allow overload" setting not surviving a reload. Base-duty, work, and faction-duty orders were valid orders the game could assign, but the save-restore normalizer only recognised follow/stay/guard/regroup/retreat/wander and silently turned anything else into the default -- so a companion on base duty or a work job came back following, its return-to order was dropped, and the overload permission reverted. The normalizer now recognises those durable orders and carries the overload setting through, so a reloaded companion keeps the job you gave it.
- Fixed bandages carried in a bag (or beyond the first 100 items) being invisible to medical care, so a companion would tear up clothing -- or refuse to be hand-bandaged -- despite having bandages in a backpack or first-aid kit. Bandage search now looks through carried containers (bounded in depth and count, with cycle detection) and uses the bandage from the container it actually lives in.
- Fixed medical care sometimes reaching for the wrong item -- e.g. a bottle of alcohol or an unrelated item whose name happened to contain "steril" -- ahead of an actual bandage. Bandage selection now proves an item is a real dressing (a bandage, ripped sheet, or something the game marks as bandage-capable) before considering sterility or alcohol as a quality bonus, so non-dressings can no longer outrank a genuine bandage. This also applies to you hand-bandaging a companion.
- Fixed companions riding as "virtual" vehicle passengers occasionally being left stranded after you got out. If placing them back into the world failed once -- no safe spot right then, or a momentary spawn hiccup -- the exit was treated as handled and they were never retried until you happened to enter and leave that vehicle again. The runtime now keeps retrying placement for a bounded window after you exit, backing off temporary failures, and no longer relaunches a placement that is already in progress. The passengers were always kept in the save; this just gets them back out of the car reliably.
- Fixed later companions being starved of decisions when several are in an emergency at once. The critical (grabbed/threatened/downed) lane scanned companions from the same fixed order every beat, so under a tight frame budget the first few always got serviced and the rest could be skipped indefinitely. The critical lane now rotates through the party, resuming where the previous beat stopped, so every emergency companion is reached in turn regardless of where the per-beat cap or budget cut off.
- Fixed a companion's spare magazines losing their ammunition on save/load. Save recorded the round count only for actual firearms, so a separate loaded magazine came back empty and the companion no longer recognised it as usable reload supply. A magazine's loaded rounds are now persisted through its own ammo count (via a verified getter/setter) and restored exactly, at the inventory root or inside a bag. Existing saves are unaffected.
- Fixed a still-living recruited companion being permanently lost when its native body failed the runtime health check for too long. Once past the grace window the companion was removed and its recovery snapshot discarded with it, so the next save simply omitted it -- a permanent loss of the survivor and everything they carried. A living recruit is now given a **recoverable retirement**: before its native actor is torn down it is preserved into the save's recovery queue (keeping its id, inventory, wounds, and orders) and re-spawned automatically once its area reloads and the actor layer is healthy again. If it cannot be preserved at all, the removal is blocked and retried rather than deleting the companion. Actual deaths still remove a companion permanently. The health view is read-only and, unlike the Status view, was gated to the same arm's-reach distance (4 tiles) as opening a companion's inventory -- so for a companion following at any normal distance the menu option silently did nothing. Viewing health/loadout does not borrow the player's loot pane (only the explicit Open Inventory action does), so that view now uses a generous distance and opens for any companion the menu offers it for.
- Added: you can now bandage a companion yourself. Right-click a nearby wounded companion and choose "Bandage companion" -- your survivor walks over and applies a bandage from your own inventory over a short timed action. The option only appears when the companion actually has a treatable wound, you are carrying a bandage, and you are close enough, so it is never a dead click; the action re-checks on completion and applies the same verified, transactional bandage the companions use on themselves.
- Kept stealth (sneak-avoidance) routing honest over a long search (review 3.5). Such a search runs across several frames against the danger picture captured when it began; a threat that died or moved off during the search kept bending the route until it finished. The search now scores against a small danger overlay that is refreshed on a throttled cadence while it runs, dropping any threat whose actor is no longer a live danger, so the chosen route reflects threats that are actually still there.
- Made companion pathfinding scale better without changing the routes it produces (review 3.3). The A* open set was a plain array scanned end to end for the lowest-cost node on every expansion (and shifted with a remove), which is quadratic as searches grow. It is now a binary min-heap ordered by cost, then heuristic, then a stable insertion sequence, so it expands nodes in the exact same order as before -- identical paths, less per-search work -- keeping route planning inside its frame budget under group load.
- Fixed a companion continuing to read as "moving" after a native path finished on its own (review 3.1). When a path ended during the engine's generic update (reached its goal, failed, or was cancelled), the bridge's path-active flag was never cleared, so isMoving/hasPendingMovement kept reporting movement and the walk state lingered. The bridge now reconciles that flag right after the generic update against the version-pinned pathfinder's own status -- clearing it once a path that moved has stopped, or once a path fails to start within a short grace window -- so the companion settles to idle when it actually stops.
- Kept companion reaction time from getting slower as the party grows (review 2.1/2.2/2.3). One decision beat used to service a single companion in round-robin, so with N companions each one only acted every N beats and an emergency waited its turn behind everyone else. A decision beat now services several companions at once (up to a per-beat cap) and runs a **critical lane** first: any companion that is grabbed, has a zombie in melee/nearby, is downed, or needs rescue is serviced every beat at a tighter cadence, capped so one permanently-threatened companion cannot starve the rest. A grab is read live so it is never gated behind the ordinary cadence. Net effect: fights and grabs get near-immediate responses regardless of squad size, while a bounded frame budget is preserved.
- Turned the per-frame native schedule-repair scan into a bounded pulse (review 2.6). Keeping companions in the cell's update list only needs re-checking occasionally -- once added they stay -- so the full roster scan now runs on a ~250 ms integrity pulse and is forced immediately whenever the roster changes (a companion is added, recovered, or removed), instead of every single frame.
- Fixed a survivor household coming up a member short when a gear item could not be created. A faction member's starting gear failed the whole member if any single item type would not instantiate (a real save could not add `Base.Book`). The member now spawns with the gear that is available and records the items it could not add, instead of aborting.
- Stopped a companion locking into a "trying to rip bandages, never doing it" loop when a treatment could not complete. With supplies present a repeatedly failing treatment had no retry cooldown, so it re-attempted every tick forever; it now backs off and gives the wound up (clearing the action) instead of looping, and logs why the treatment animation did not complete so the underlying cause is diagnosable.
- Bounded the treatment approach so a companion that can never reach its patient gives up instead of navigating forever. A live-sandbox probe reproduced a treatment that returned "arrived" every tick (500+ times in nine seconds) because an unreachable or position-unavailable patient reads an infinite distance, so it never settled within medical range to start the bandage. The approach now times out after a budget (default 8s) and clears the treatment, closing another path into the same rip-loop symptom.
- Dead companions no longer linger in the infection-crisis list. A crisis whose subject has died or turned is resolved, and the active list now hides resolved crises (they remain in the crisis history), so only ongoing infections show.
- Leaving base duty now cancels any in-flight base job. A companion switched from base duty to follow no longer carries a queued, blocked base task (such as a barricade) around in the field -- the claim is released and the queued action cleared.
- Stopped the stomp/floor-attack path from spamming the log with a caught "attempted index of non-table" error every time it aimed at or cleared a downed target. It assigned the native targetOnGround field directly, which Kahlua cannot do; it now drives that field through a bridge setter (the same pattern used for the other native fields), so the downed-target reference is set and cleared cleanly.
- Fixed companions moonwalking -- playing the forward walk/run cycle while sliding backward or sideways -- when strafing, kiting, or backpedaling. The stock directional strafe animation was only used when a caller explicitly flagged the move as tactical; it now engages whenever the companion travels off its facing axis, which is the actual condition for backward/side animation.
- Fixed companions running off the map when retreating. A combat retreat preferred a remembered egress route (its far map-entry point) with no distance limit, so a single retreat could sprint clear across the map. A retreat now caps its distance and falls back to the nearby escape square when the remembered target is too far, keeping it a local break-contact move.
- Fixed a following companion running straight past you when you turn, continuing until a wall stops it. Its native movement path committed to a goal captured when the path began and only re-aimed once that goal drifted three tiles away (or a 6.5s lease expired), so a leader turning -- especially to run along a wall it was heading toward -- left the companion pathing into the old direction. A path chasing a moving target (following the player) now re-aims on a much smaller drift and holds a much shorter lease, so companions track your turns instead of overshooting.
- Fixed a teleport-recovered companion running off in a straight line (or not moving well) after being pulled back to follow the player. A recovery teleport dropped the companion onto a new square but left its navigation state -- native path lease, computed path, goal square, route memory, blocked edges -- pointing at the pre-teleport route, so it walked the stale path from its new position. Recovery now resets the navigation state, so the companion replans fresh from where it landed.
- Fixed every companion showing wounds on all body parts (all at severity 30) and trying to change bandages over its whole body forever, which filled its work queue and left it standing still (playtest 4 / "all limbs damaged"). Wound assessment read `BodyPart.IsInfected()`, which reports the character's Knox (zombie) infection -- the engine propagates that flag to every body part -- so a Knox-infected companion saw its entire body as infected. It now reads only the local, treatable wound infection (`isInfectedWound`); Knox remains handled by the character-level infection check and the infection-crisis system.
- Fixed companions reloading with their backpack left in inventory instead of on their back, and a follow-on vanilla `refreshBackpacks` error. A bag has no `getBodyLocation()` (that is for worn clothing); its worn slot comes from `canBeEquipped()`. Worn-item restore (persistence and logistics) now resolves the slot from `canBeEquipped()` when `getBodyLocation()` is absent, and no longer mistakes a reflection miss for a valid location, so bags are re-worn instead of dropped to inventory.
- Stopped a large squad from oscillating combat targets by giving each fighter a proper engagement lease on its zombie (review 4.4). The old ~450 ms claim expired between an actor's combat decisions once the party grew, so two companions would trade the same target back and forth. The lease now spans the worst-case time before a fighter is serviced again (~2 s), is refreshed on every offensive action, and is released the instant its owner switches away, disengages, or dies -- so a committed attacker holds its target while it is engaging without stranding a claim on an abandoned or dead one.
- Made starting an action atomic with dispatching a queued urgent intent (review 2.5). Beginning a companion action first services the actor, which can dispatch a waiting urgent intent (like an emergency retreat) whose handler takes ownership; the same call could then stack or preempt a normal action on top of the urgent it had just launched. Beginning an action now detects that an urgent was just dispatched and defers to it, so exactly one owner starts per urgent dispatch.
- Gave each companion decision a stable identity so "patch myself", "patch the player" and "patch a hurt ally" are no longer treated as the same choice (review 2.4). They previously all counted as the generic "medical" decision, so the state-stickiness that prevents flip-flopping -- and the fallback that tries a lesser option when the best one has no action -- could not tell them apart, and a companion that couldn't treat itself would not fall back to helping someone else. Each candidate now carries a distinct key (kind plus mode and target), so hysteresis and fallback act on the exact decision.
- Surfaced the native swing/floor-attack collision capability instead of failing it silently (review 4.3). The bridge could report ready while the combat-collision reflection was missing, which left a combat companion swinging without ever landing a hit and no way to tell why. The bridge now exposes explicit combat-collision and floor-attack (stomp) capability flags; the support/health view reports a degraded combat state and warns once when the collision path is unavailable, and a real-JAR test asserts both capabilities resolve on the shipped build. A missing floor attack degrades only the stomp, not general melee or ranged combat.
- Made incoming zombie attacks edge-triggered so each swing wounds a companion exactly once (review 4.7). A zombie's attack state -- and its "success" outcome afterwards -- stays true for many frames, so the previous per-frame check re-applied the same swing repeatedly, held off only by a fixed time cooldown that in turn swallowed a genuinely fast second swing. Each swing is now resolved once on its rising edge and held until the zombie leaves the attack, so a slow attacker can't double-wound and a fast attacker's second swing still lands.
- Fixed zombies walking up to a companion and then just standing there instead of attacking. The code that keeps a zombie's "recently seen target" clock fresh for a non-local companion (stock vision only refreshes it against the local player array) wrote the native field directly, which throws in Kahlua every frame and silently never applied — so the zombie kept lapsing out of its swing. It now drives the native `setTargetSeenTime` setter, so zombies commit to attacks (and bites) on companions again.
- Fixed a companion's menu roster entry breaking (and spamming the log every UI refresh) once it had accumulated enough relationship memories. The roster summary copied the companion's entire memories array, which is nested one level deeper than the summary's copy depth and grew past its value budget, so the bounded-copy guard threw "stable value limit exceeded" on every refresh at around thirteen memories -- leaving that companion's roster data unavailable. No summary reader used the array (the memory view reads the live memories directly), so the summary now carries just a bounded memory count.
- Fixed a teleport-recovered companion being permanently despawned ("body never shows up again"). When a follower that had unloaded was teleported back to the player, the runtime health gate re-checked native validity on the same tick and, if it had not yet settled, removed the companion outright with no retry. Native validity dips transiently for a few frames after a teleport re-seats the actor on a fresh square, so the health gate now tolerates a bounded settling window (default 1.5s) and only tears down a companion whose native state stays broken past it -- a genuinely dead actor is still retired immediately by the death path.
- Fixed companions freezing for minutes at a fence or wall after teleporting in to follow the player. A non-local companion teleported mid-climb never finishes the climb animation, so it stayed in a native "climbing" state that navigation could only wait on -- looping forever without moving. A climb that outlives its timeout now exits through Build 42's normal `PlayerActionsState`, invoking the vanilla climb state's cleanup for collision and animation flags, verifies that `isClimbing()` cleared, temporarily blacklists the unfinished edge, and reroutes. A rejected native transition receives only bounded attempts before the route enters terminal recovery instead of spinning forever.
- Fixed companions freezing permanently in a "kneel treat"/"rip clothing" pose after a medical action was interrupted (reported as "Urgent already queued, just stands still locked up" and companions stuck at treat-wound). An interrupted native visual action left a dead record that kept reporting as a live activity, so the urgent-decision path re-queued forever and the supervisor's urgent dispatch stayed blocked, while the interrupted kneel pose was never reset. A stopped/interrupted visual is now self-healed the moment its status is read: it is cleared (which also resets the model pose) and reported as idle, so the companion re-decides and moves instead of locking up.
- Added one actor-wide supervisor for movement and timed actions, with explicit ownership, completion, cancellation, retry, rollback, and support evidence.
- Fixed drawn melee weapons failing to swing: the bridge now exposes and verifies Build 42.20.4's native `AttackType`, the action adapter performs the real player attack preflight/authorization, and melee followers safely close the final gap instead of kiting forever.
- Fixed companion (and hostile-faction NPC) melee swings connecting but dealing no damage: Build 42's swing-collision anim event only resolves a hit for the local player, so a non-local actor animated and found targets but never applied damage. The native actor now drives the collision check itself on that event, using the `AttackType` the swing animation carries so the hit passes the engine's stance filter and lands.
- Companions now clear a jammed firearm by racking it (the same action the player's firearm menu uses) before firing or reloading, instead of standing on a dead gun. Verified end to end alongside firing, magazine reloads, and reusing a reloaded weapon.
- Restricted the "catch up to the player" chunk-unload recovery to following companions. A companion set to stay, guard, or base duty is no longer teleported across the map when the player walks out of range; its record is kept intact so it reloads at its post when the player returns.
- Fixed a teleport-recovered companion appearing to vanish: it still showed on the minimap, could be looted, and drew zombies to its tile, but stood invisible and never moved. The recovery restored the actor's square and render model but never returned it to the cell object list the update scheduler iterates, so it was never ticked (which also let its render alpha fade out). Recovery now reschedules the actor, so it moves and stays visible immediately after a teleport.
- Fixed zombies ignoring companions — following them but never biting. Build 42's zombie vision only scans the local `players[]` array, so a detached companion is never "seen": its target-lock decayed every frame (the zombie just walked toward it) and, even when a swing resolved, the bite is written by the victim's local-player-gated processing that never runs for a non-local actor. Companions now refresh the same seen-flesh timer continuous vision keeps (holding the lock without altering zombie AI) and take real BodyDamage wounds from a landed attack — bites can infect and turn them, while scratches and lacerations wound and bleed without infecting.
- Streamlined the companion panel from nine tabs to five (Status, Orders, Loadout, Base, More): merged the Gear and Health tabs into one Loadout page, moved Groups/Factions/Journal/Support behind a "More" launcher, dropped status readouts that duplicated other tabs, and collapsed the two overlapping combat controls (a per-companion stance selector plus a separate team doctrine selector) into a single per-companion combat selector with an "Apply to whole squad" button. The doctrine still cascades to the underlying stance and hold-fire, so behavior and saves are unchanged.
- Added zombie pull-downs: when enough zombies pile onto a companion at once, they can grab and drag it to the ground (resisted by the companion's traits), pinning it while it is torn at. A pinned companion cannot act and cries out for help; there is a grace window to save it — thin the swarm and it is freed, bloodied but alive; leave it and the pack drags it down for good.
- Companions now finish a downed zombie with a head stomp: they step onto the zombie's head square (`getHeadSquare`) and stomp the skull — the lethal spot — for a clean kill, while a stomp landed anywhere else only wounds and keeps it pinned. The finisher's damage is applied once, only after the native swing has actually started, so a rejected or retried stomp never harms the target, and it is the sole damage owner (Build 42's floor attack never resolves a downed target for a non-local actor).
- Hardened the companion↔local-player boundary and grab handling after an external code review: the engine's standalone `postupdate()` pass now runs inside the same local-player snapshot/restore as `update()` (an unguarded post-update pass could leak player-indexed ownership and disturb the real player's hotbar/inventory), while nested post-update calls stay under `update()`'s existing guard so the multi-frame swing animation is left intact; a companion that boards a vehicle or is removed/reset while grabbed now has its pin cleared so its decisions resume; and the zombie pull-down "pile" only counts zombies actually committed to that companion (tolerating brief target flicker) instead of every zombie in range, so a swarm attacking the player no longer drags a bystanding companion down.
- Companion timed actions (work/reload/etc.) are cancelled by removing only the Living-Fellows-owned action instance instead of clearing the whole native timed-action queue, so an unrelated mod's action queued on the companion is never dropped.
- Made the global `ISInventoryPage` container hooks argument- and return-safe: the `selectContainer`/`setNewContainer` wrappers now forward every argument and preserve every return value (including multiple returns), so a Build update or another inventory/hotbar mod that adds parameters or returns extra values is no longer silently corrupted.
- Companions are no longer frozen in place by low health. Knox infection and injuries now just lower a companion's health and it keeps acting like a player — moving, fighting, fleeing, and being bandaged — until it dies at zero health or turns at terminal Knox, instead of being forced into an immobile "downed" state at low health (which also left it standing frozen rather than reacting). Any companion still stuck in a legacy downed state stands back up.
- Fixed companions and faction members reloading naked (their clothing left in inventory): Build 42's `setWornItem` takes an `ItemBodyLocation` object, but restore/equip/bandage code passed the persisted location string, so the native call found no matching overload and silently dropped every worn item. Those calls now resolve the location from the item's own `getBodyLocation()`, so saved clothing (and bandages) are actually re-worn on load.
- Companions no longer decide to reload on ammunition they cannot actually use: the reload check now matches a magazine of the weapon's exact magazine type (that still holds rounds) or loose rounds of its exact ammo type, instead of accepting any item whose name merely contains "ammo"/"bullets"/"shells" — which previously matched the wrong calibre or an empty magazine and produced repeated failed reload attempts.
- A companion that starts to swing but has the attack rejected during preflight (equip not ready, weapon not attack-ready, authorization unavailable, etc.) no longer stays unnaturally locked facing a stale target: every rejected attack now clears the precise aim target, floor-aim, and downed-target state through one structured cleanup path instead of leaving the native actor re-facing the old target every frame.
- Borrowing the player's loot pane to show a companion's inventory is now a reversible transaction: the pane's prior container, visibility and collapsed state are snapshotted and put back when the companion panel closes or minimizes or you leave the Loadout tab — but only while the pane still shows the companion container, so a container you deliberately opened afterwards is left alone.
- Fixed the intermittent companion vanish (and the "flaky" ranged/melee failures that were its downstream symptoms) at the root: the per-frame locomotion speed refresh (`updateMovementRates` → `calculateWalkSpeed` → `calculateSneakLimpSpeedScale`) reads a limp/sneak animation-variable slot that is momentarily null on the non-local companion **while it is limping from a leg wound**, throwing every frame, failing the whole native update, and tripping the health gate into a remove+respawn. That non-critical speed refresh is now guarded, so a limping companion keeps updating instead of disappearing. Verified with eight consecutive live-sandbox runs: zero removals, zero animation NPEs, ranged fully reliable (previously it failed roughly one run in four to six).
- Melee companions now swing from a distance keyed to their weapon's actual reach instead of a fixed gap: a short blade closes in to connect while a long blade holds a safer gap and still lands, and a companion backs off to reopen its swing range when a target crowds inside it — so it keeps kiting distance yet actually hits, instead of missing with short weapons or over-closing with long ones.
- Fixed on-foot ranged companions drawing and swinging a melee weapon: the ranged-support doctrine now selects the firearm whether or not the companion is seated, so an approaching zombie no longer flips the pick to melee when the saved weapon priority is stale.
- Fixed companions becoming invisible while still present (visible only on the minimap, "body never returns" after a teleport or chunk reload): the native actor now forces full render opacity each update, since the vanilla visibility fade never raised its alpha.
- Fixed companions being under-simulated and, when stationary, effectively frozen (idle animations, attacks that started but never landed, "just standing there doing nothing"): a manually constructed non-local actor was only placed in the cell's deferred add list, so Build 42's update scheduler stopped ticking it. The runtime now keeps every live companion in the cell object list each frame, so they are simulated like normal characters and melee swings resolve their hit.
- Fixed a companion vanishing mid-fight and returning as a fresh, unequipped body: a per-frame native diagnostic read an animation variable whose slot is momentarily null on the non-local actor, and the resulting exception failed the runtime health gate, which removed and respawned the companion. That read is now guarded, and a recoverable native-update exception is tolerated for a bounded window (the local player is always restored first) instead of tearing the companion down on the first hiccup.
- Made medical care, downtime, scavenging, logistics, vehicle seating, equipment, and loadout changes transactional instead of applying partial effects.
- Made in-combat self-bandaging low priority: a companion no longer stops to patch itself while a zombie is attacking it in melee range (or while pinned with no way out). It fights or repositions first and treats the wound only once at least semi-safe; a critical wound still drops it into the downed path.

- Replaced partial save copies with strict path-aware copies. Uncopyable envelopes block overwrite, while copyable invalid records and failed subsystem documents remain quarantined and recoverable without losing their untouched raw value.
